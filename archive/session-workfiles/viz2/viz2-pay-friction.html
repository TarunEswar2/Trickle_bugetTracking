Pay friction

You pick a category and an amount. On the pay screen the boxes that payment takes crumble out of that category's grid, and a ghost of what left stays until you move on. Below are four ways to crumble and three ways to draw the ghost, tried on all six students. Options only. You decide.

Why these choices

  • Soman 2001: past payments only curb spending when the amount is rehearsed and the money leaves at once. The crumble happens at the moment of paying, so the loss is immediate and visible.
  • Knutson et al. 2007: the felt cost is strongest when the price is in front of you at the decision. Showing the loss before you confirm puts it there.
  • Tversky & Kahneman 1981 and Olafsson & Pagel 2018: lead with what is left and keep alarm out. The words say what remains; over is amber, never red.
  • Garcia-Retamero 2010: keep the whole visible. The ghost keeps the departed boxes in the grid, so the payment is seen against the budget.

Decisions for Tarun

1. How do the boxes crumble?

  1. Break into chunks that fall a short way where they stand (A).
  2. Drop out through the bottom of the grid (B).
  3. Turn to grains that drift off (C).
  4. Fade away one by one, top first (D).

2. How does the ghost look?

  1. Dashed outline where the liquid was (G2).
  2. Pale fill (G1).
  3. Pale fill plus a small "this payment" tag (G3).

3. Payment smaller than one box?

  1. Crumble its true size: a sliver of a box (as in D).
  2. No crumble below one box; the words line says it and nothing else.
At ₹250 a box on a monthly grid a ₹20 chai is under a tenth of a box. Category grids show it, the monthly one cannot.

4. Confirm for going over

  1. Everything left crumbles, then the amber floor line and words appear (V1-4 carried to the pay screen).
  2. Something else.

5. Changing the amount or category

  1. The ghost updates live and snaps back if you back out.
  2. The crumble only plays on the final amount.
