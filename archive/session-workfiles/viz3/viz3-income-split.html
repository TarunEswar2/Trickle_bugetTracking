Income split

Your income is one grid. It splits into spending and savings, spending splits into budget and subscriptions, savings split into goals, and the budget splits into its categories. It is the same grid at every step. Below: how the split can sit in the grid, how you go deeper, and what happens at 2 and at 18 categories. Options only. You decide.

Why these choices

  • Garcia-Retamero 2010: show the whole. The grid is always the whole income, so every part is seen against it.
  • Neurath / Kinross and Cleveland & McGill: repeat one unit and compare on a common edge. Every region is made of the same box, and bands share a floor.
  • Kay et al. 2016: around 20 countable marks work, 100 read as a level. The grid reads as shares, and the scale line gives the magnitude.
  • Principle 6: savings is what unspent money becomes. Savings is a region of the same grid, not a separate screen.

Decisions for Tarun

1. How does the split sit in the grid?

  1. Bands from the bottom, row by row (A).
  2. Columns from the left (B).

2. Which is at the bottom (or left)?

  1. Savings, with spending on top.
  2. Spending at the bottom, savings on top.

3. How do you go deeper?

  1. Tap a colour or its name and it splits in place, one level at a time.
  2. Everything at once: goals, subscriptions and categories all showing.

4. Budget against subscriptions

  1. Budget light orange, subscriptions hatched orange.
  2. Both the same orange with a line between.

5. Many categories

  1. The biggest five get a colour, the rest share one, tap to open the full list.
  2. Every category its own colour, colours repeat after about eight.

6. A group with nothing in it

  1. No subscriptions or no goals: that region simply isn't there.
  2. Show an empty named region ("no goal yet").
