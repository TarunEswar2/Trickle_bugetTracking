# Trickle — Stage 0: Facts to build on (APPROVED 2 Oct 2026)

Sources: research/brymans_analysis_interviews.md (6 interviews, Bryman 4-step coding), Tarun's ongoing user conversations (2 Oct 2026), claude/project_master_synthesis.md.

## Conclusion of the primary research (Tarun's words)
The key challenge is the gap between making a payment and perceiving its accumulative impact. Digital payments make spending convenient; students spend more than usual in certain situations; tracking is difficult. Students need to understand spending more easily and keep control without feeling restricted.
Pattern: Spending is easy → Tracking is difficult → Awareness comes late.

## Six themes (Bryman)
1. Varied approaches to money management (no budget, mental limits, purpose accounts, buffers).
2. Spending is situational (social outings, quick-commerce add-ons, offers, free-delivery thresholds, impulse, big episodes).
3. Small everyday purchases become significant through accumulation (5 of 6).
4. Awareness comes after the money is spent (5 of 6).
5. Tracking is too much effort; students want control without losing discretionary spending.
6. Students are developing awareness and control (daily/weekly balance checks, goals, category budgets, compensating after big spends).

## Specific evidence worth designing for

## Tarun's user-input facts (2 Oct 2026)
- Income splits into spending and savings. If you don't spend, you save more.
- 1 dot = ₹100 is easy to understand; the problem is dots combining into lines and boxes. Not combining makes large amounts hard to show. → unresolved representation problem.
- Showing small purchases feels a bit like nagging, but it builds awareness and long-term habits → keep it.
- Telling someone they spend more at night is important (awareness).
- Telling someone a subscription will take money in 2 days is important.
- Building awareness is the goal. Bringing back the friction of paying is important.
- No big number on Home: seeing "₹200 left, you're doing badly" makes people close and uninstall.
- Tracking repeat purchases is important.
- Tabs: Home, Income, Spending (budget setting, how much spent, transaction history), Savings (how much, by when, how much actually saved).
- Progressive disclosure is key.

## Principles (approved as written, 2 Oct)
1. Awareness, not restriction — show patterns and the cost of a payment; never forbid or scold.
2. Show money at the moment it matters — at payment, before a subscription hits, when a pattern repeats.
3. Gentle on open — Home never leads with a big or bad number; detail on tap.
4. Zero-effort tracking — UPI link or quick manual entry.
5. Mental limits over rigid budgets — budgets feel like the "up to ₹1,000" people already use.
6. Savings is what unspent money becomes — not spending is shown as saving.
7. Patterns over raw history — repeat buys, nights, situations, small buys adding up.

## Decided 2 Oct
- The 7 principles are approved as written.
- 5 tabs: Home · Income · Spending · Savings · Insights.
- Stage order: money representation first (Stage 2), then onboarding, insights, screens.
