One tile. ₹100. Every screen.

The system behind the Trickle v11 prototype: the tile, colours, type, components, motion, sound and words, plus a test script for five students. Money comes from linked UPI IDs or manual entry only.

The tile

■ = ₹100, always. Rows of 10 with a gap after 5, so a row reads as ₹1,000 and a half row as ₹500. Amounts under ₹100 are a partial tile that fills from the bottom. Above 100 tiles, rows group into 10×10 squares of ₹10,000; a bigger glyph is never used. The key "■ = ₹100" appears once per screen, on the first tile visual.

Palette

Neutrals carry a slight green bias. Category hues come in a fixed order and none is red. Validator (dark on #1b1d1c): all checks pass, worst CVD ΔE 8.4, worst normal-vision ΔE 19.3, contrast ≥3:1. In light mode, Fun, Study and Other get a contrast warning, so every jar row carries its name.

Components

Tile bar (jar)

Left filled in the jar's colour, spent outlined after it. Tap for exact ₹. Used on Money, jar detail and the pay moment.

Split bar

2-column grid, 10 px gap, radius 24. Title bottom-left, settings glyph top-right. Home holds up to 6 cards.

Sheet

A bottom sheet with radius 28 and a grab bar. It holds one fact, one primary button that says what happens, and at most one text link. Used for empty jar, split, money in, price change and the weekly check-in.

Tab bar

A floating pill: Home · Money · Pay (filled, centre) · Insights. Settings sits in a header button. Screens pad 120 px at the bottom so nothing sits under the bar.

All sound goes through a 2.4 kHz lowpass and a compressor, with each voice peaking at 0.15 or lower. Sound is on by default, follows the phone's silent switch, and has one toggle in Settings.

Copy rules

  1. Talk like a friend who is good with money. Use second person and short sentences.
  2. Use the words students use: money in, spending money, jars, set aside, left over, move tiles, friends owe you.
  3. Never write: over, overspent, deficit, carried, debt, cover, sweep, pool, assign, invariant, warning, alert, failed, "you should". The built file is checked with a grep.
  4. Give the facts, then one choice. Use no exclamation marks, except one on a savings milestone.
  5. Write ₹ with Indian grouping and no decimals. Write the key as "■ = ₹100".
  6. Buttons say what happens ("Split it", "Take from Food"). Never "OK" or "Submit".
  7. Home never shows a number. Other screens show at most two on first view, with exact ₹ on tap. The one exception is the price-change sheet, whose three numbers come from the Phase 8 copy.

Usability test: 5 students

Recruit five Indian college students aged 18–24 who pay with UPI: two in hostels, one day scholar, one renting, one earning. Each session takes 25 minutes on a phone, with a think-aloud and a stopwatch. Open the prototype fresh for tasks 2–3, and with #demo for the rest.
