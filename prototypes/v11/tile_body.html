Why F wins

  • One fact to learn. "A tile is ₹100" is a round number every Indian student already handles as a note. Rows of 10 give ₹1,000 for free (decimal place value, like an abacus rod) without a second glyph.
  • Glance. The 5|5 gap turns a row into two subitizable fives, so 37 tiles read as "3 rows + 5 + 2" in about 1.8 s instead of 11 s of counting.
  • Never changes. Unlike the day-tile (B) and 1% (C), the value survives a budget edit, a new month and a screenshot shared with a friend.
  • Honest. Area is proportional to rupees everywhere; sub-₹100 amounts are a partially filled tile (₹25 chai = a quarter tile), never a different shape.
  • Runner-up D (ten-frames) reads as fast but packs rows into 2×5 blocks, which breaks the left-to-right "row = ₹1,000" story and wraps badly in narrow cards. B (day tile) is the most meaningful per tile but fails S7: its value changes whenever the budget does, which is v9's failure again.

Rules that come with the tile

  • Key format, always the same: a filled tile, "= ₹100". Shown once per screen, bottom-left of the first tile visual.
  • Max 100 tiles (10 rows, ₹10,000) per card. Above that, show a ₹10,000 square of 10×10 as a group, never a bigger tile.
  • Below ₹100: a partial tile filled from the bottom. Below ₹10: a sliver, with the exact ₹ on tap.
  • Leave the tile for time (hour-of-day, calendar position) and for trend lines; those are positions, not amounts.
