Trickle v10 · concept board

Shape is how much.
Colour is what.

Every rupee Trickle draws becomes a small shape from one six-step ladder. The same ₹100 triangle appears in your balance, a payment, a goal and a chart. Colour only ever says which pot the money belongs to.

1 · The ladder

Six glyphs, one square cell

Each glyph lives in a 24-unit cell with a 2-unit round-joined stroke in its own fill colour, which rounds every corner and gives all six the same edge weight. Sizes are set by optical area, not geometry: the triangle sits 1 unit low so it looks centred, the diamond runs to the edge because its area is small, and the ₹1000 circle is a coin (ring plus centre) so it never reads as a big ₹10 dot.

Colour · large · 24px · 12px
Black & white mode

In B&W, shape still carries the amount. Categories switch to four brightness steps plus a text label next to every group.

12px stress test · ₹6,350 in a list row

Star, circle, diamond, triangle, square and dot stay distinct at 12px because each has a different silhouette class: points, ring, 4-corner, 3-corner, flat edges, small blob.

2 · Rules

How the shapes behave

Colour vs shape

Shape = amount, always from the ladder. Colour = what: a category, Budget (bone), Savings (lime), New money (green). No shape ever changes meaning between screens.

Amounts use mixed piles

A single amount (balance, payment, goal, income) breaks down greedily into the biggest glyphs first and is always sorted star → dot. Tap a pile to see the exact ₹.

Comparisons use one shape

Charts that compare things draw every bar in one glyph, the chart's unit, chosen from the ladder so the biggest bar needs ≤ 40 glyphs. A key sits on top: "▲ = ₹100". Counting is honest.

Partial amounts

Visuals round to the nearest ₹10. ₹37 draws 4 dots (₹40); ₹34 draws 3. Amounts under ₹5 draw one hollow dot so a payment is never invisible. Exact ₹ shows on tap and in text.

Max glyph counts

Mixed pile: ≤ 24 glyphs (greedy keeps a ₹9,990 pile at 17). Above ₹50,000 the pile shows stars only with a "+" count. Comparison chart: ≤ 40 per bar. Rows in lists: ≤ 8 glyphs at 12px.

Not money? Plain dots

Days, times, counts of buys and streaks use plain neutral dots with no ladder meaning, so a dot of colour never pretends to be ₹10.

5 · Open questions

Still to decide

  1. ₹10 floor for students. Most chai and auto spends are ₹10–₹40, so rows fill with dots. Should the ladder add a ₹20 step, or is "4 dots" fine because small spends should look small?
  2. Coin vs dot confusion at 12px. The ring is 1.25px thick at 12px. On low-DPI Android screens do we bump list glyphs to 14px minimum?
  3. Rounding a pile vs the jar. If visuals round to ₹10, a jar at ₹4 shows one hollow dot but three ₹4 jars would show three dots (₹30 implied). Accept, or show "under ₹10" as text only?
  4. Per-shape notes during big piles. A ₹9,990 income plays 17 notes. Cap at the first 6 and then a single chord, or one note per denomination class?
